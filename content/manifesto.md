# Manifesto: from ideas to decisive execution

## 1. The problem is not invention

The field has produced an abundance of validated techniques.  The missing ingredient is not creativity.  The missing ingredient is integrated, scaled, schedule-driven execution.

## 2. Integration is the main physics risk

When multiple “best-in-class” subsystems are combined, new failure modes appear:

- cross-contamination and handling complexity,
- additional materials and services that reintroduce backgrounds,
- calibration and stability constraints that couple subsystems,
- manufacturing yield and QA that do not scale linearly,
- analysis complexity that erodes effective exposure.

Integration is not a footnote.  It is the experiment.

## 3. Scale converts plausibility into certainty

At small scale, every program can look competitive.  At large scale, only a small number of execution paths remain viable.

Scale forces clarity:

- what is buildable,
- what is maintainable,
- what is testable end-to-end,
- what can be delivered on a timeline that matters.

## 4. Capital is an engineering input

Raising capital is part of execution.  It determines team structure, procurement strategy, test infrastructure, and schedule realism.

If we want the experimental answer soon, funding must be treated as a first-class subsystem with owners, milestones, and measurable outputs.

## 5. The ask

Support a program that treats $0\nu\beta\beta$ as an integration-and-scale engineering project.  Measure success by integrated demonstrations, credible scaling paths, and schedule discipline.
